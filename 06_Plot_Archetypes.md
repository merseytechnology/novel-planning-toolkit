# 6. Plot Archetypes

> The deep-structure pattern your story is built on. Naming it early makes it easier to spot what your plot is missing — or where it's fighting itself.
>
> ## Reference: seven basic plots
>
> *Most novels lean on one primary archetype, sometimes blended with a second. None of these are formulas to follow exactly — they're patterns to measure your instincts against.*
>
> | Archetype | Core pattern |
> | --- | --- |
> | **Overcoming the Monster** | a hero sets out to defeat a threatening force before it destroys their world. |
> | **Rags to Riches** | an overlooked figure rises, is tested by a fall, and earns a truer version of success. |
> | **The Quest** | a hero and companions travel to obtain a prize, facing escalating trials along the way. |
> | **Voyage and Return** | a hero is thrown into a strange world, survives it, and returns changed by what they saw. |
> | **Comedy** | confusion and misunderstanding build until a final revelation clears the way to unity or resolution. |
> | **Tragedy** | a flaw or desire drives the protagonist toward a downfall that feels, in hindsight, inevitable. |
> | **Rebirth** | a protagonist trapped by circumstance or their own darkness is redeemed by an outside force. |
>
> > **Worth remembering**
> > > An archetype is a skeleton, not a script. The best use of this page is diagnostic: if a draft feels shapeless, checking it against the nearest archetype often reveals which beat is missing — not a beat to copy in.
