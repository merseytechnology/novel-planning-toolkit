# 7. Plot Endings

> How your story lands. An ending is where every promise the book made gets paid off — or deliberately withheld.
>
> ## Reference: common ending types
>
> *Endings often combine more than one of these. Use this list to name what you're reaching for before you write toward it.*
>
> | Ending type | What it does |
> | --- | --- |
> | **Resolved / Happy** | the protagonist gets what they wanted, or what they needed, or both. |
> | **Tragic** | the protagonist fails, loses, or is destroyed by the same flaw that drove the story. |
> | **Bittersweet** | a real win costs something that can't be gotten back — victory and loss at once. |
> | **Twist** | a late revelation recasts everything the reader thought they understood. |
> | **Open / Ambiguous** | the outcome is left for the reader to decide, on purpose. |
> | **Circular** | the final image echoes the opening one — same place, changed person. |
> | **Cliffhanger** | the central conflict resolves, but a new one opens the door to what's next. |
